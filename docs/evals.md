# Eval guide

The evals simulate a user turn by turn against the deterministic scripted
model and seeded mock provider API. Each scenario starts with fresh temporary
state. Assertions focus on outcomes and invariants, such as whether a booking
exists, whether its slot meets the user's constraints, and whether approval was
checked. They do not require exact response wording and do not call a live LLM.

Run all scenarios from the repository root:

```bash
python3 -m evals.run
```

This regenerates `eval_report.md` with the scenario, expected result, observed
result, pass/fail status and notes. The 23 scenarios cover:

1. **Normal booking and injection:** a filtered booking respects budget and
   time constraints; an injected provider review cannot change intent or
   bypass approval.
2. **Approval changes:** a changed quote or corrected slot voids the pending
   approval and leaves the agent without an unintended booking.
3. **Booking recovery:** timeout-after-success is recovered through the
   authoritative status lookup; timeout-without-success can be retried within
   bounds; a taken slot is reported without claiming success.
4. **Other booking actions:** reschedule conflicts leave the original booking
   unchanged; cancellation handles the required transient failure and retry.
5. **Repeated actions and approval invariants:** repeated “yes” and replayed
   idempotency keys do not create duplicates; no booking occurs without
   approval.
6. **Memory:** stated preferences survive a new session, corrections replace
   old values, forgotten facts are not reused, and inferred preferences require
   confirmation before use.
7. **Restart and limits:** a mid-booking restart checks status before retrying;
   exhausted model turns produce a clear stop instead of a partial booking.
8. **Conversation continuity:** the agent asks only for missing details,
   preserves known slots after corrections, and applies confirmed technician
   preferences with their constraints.
9. **Additional regression cases:** intervening bookings, forgotten
   preferences across restart, and stray input at a yes/no prompt do not break
   the workflow.

The full scenario definitions and their assertions are in `evals/scenarios.py`;
`eval_report.md` is generated by `evals/run.py`.
